Featured · Rāshidūn Caliph I

Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq

أَبُو بَكْرٍ الصِّدِّيقُ

Born c. 573 CE, Makkah Died 23 Jumādā al-Ākhirah 13 AH / 634 CE, Madīnah Companion of the Prophet ﷺ

ʿAbdullāh ibn Abī Quḥāfah, known by his kunyah Abū Bakr and the epithet aṣ-Ṣiddīq ("the Truthful"), was among the first free adult men to accept Islam. A modest cloth merchant of the Banū Taym clan of Quraysh, he was the Prophet ﷺ's closest friend before and after prophethood.

He accompanied the Prophet ﷺ on the Hijrah, hidden with him in the cave of Thawr — a moment preserved in Qur'ān 9:40. He led the ḥajj in 9 AH, and after the Prophet's death in 11 AH was elected the first caliph, holding the community together through the Riddah wars and initiating the compilation of the Qur'ān as one text.

His caliphate lasted only two years and three months. He was buried beside the Prophet ﷺ in ʿĀʾishah's chamber.

Sources: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3653–3691 (Manāqib Abī Bakr) · Ibn Hishām, as-Sīrah an-Nabawiyyah · adh-Dhahabī, Siyar aʿlām an-nubalāʾ · Ibn Kathīr, al-Bidāyah wa-n-Nihāyah

Caliph I

Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq

أَبُو بَكْرٍ الصِّدِّيقُ

c. 573 – 634 CE. First caliph. The Prophet's ﷺ companion in the cave of Thawr. Led the Riddah wars; initiated the compilation of the Qur'ān.

Bukhārī 3653–3691 · Ibn Kathīr, Bidāyah vol. 6
ع
Caliph II

ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb

عُمَر بْن الْخَطَّاب

c. 584 – 644 CE. Second caliph. Al-Fārūq — "the one who distinguishes." Established the Hijri calendar, the ʿaṭāʾ registers, and much of the early administrative order.

Bukhārī 3689 · adh-Dhahabī, Siyar · Ibn Saʿd, Ṭabaqāt
ع
Caliph III

ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān

عُثْمَان بْن عَفَّان

c. 576 – 656 CE. Third caliph. "Dhū an-Nūrayn" — "the one of two lights" — for marrying two of the Prophet's ﷺ daughters. Standardized the muṣḥaf into the ʿUthmānī rasm still used today.

Bukhārī 3695–3706 · Ibn Kathīr, Bidāyah vol. 7
ع
Caliph IV

ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib

عَلِي بْن أَبِي طَالِب

c. 600 – 661 CE. Fourth caliph. Cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ. The first child to embrace Islam. Renowned for eloquence, judgment, and courage.

Bukhārī 3701 · Muslim 2404 · Nahj al-Balāghah (attrib.)
