The Verse of the Day · Al-Baqarah 2:255

The Throne Verse

Perhaps the most-recited single verse of the Qur'ān — on protection, wakefulness, and the incomparability of God.

A translation with notes · By the editors

ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلْحَىُّ ٱلْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُۥ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ

Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwal-ḥayyul-qayyūm · lā ta'khudhuhu sinatun wa lā nawm.

"Allāh — there is no god but Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him, nor sleep."

— Translation: Ṣaḥīḥ International

Of the six thousand two hundred and thirty-six āyāt of the Qur'ān, no single verse is recited more often than this one. It is said after every obligatory prayer. It is whispered before sleep. It is written on doorposts, on amulets, on the covers of children's books. Its density is astonishing: a full theology folded into a single arc of grammar.

What returns, again and again, is the negation. "There is no god" — and then the exception. The verse refuses easy analogies. It does not compare God to a king (though God is sovereign), nor to a father (though God is compassionate). It compares God to nothing at all, because nothing is comparable. What is offered instead is a series of attributes — Living, Sustaining, unforgetful, unslumbering, unwearied — that the reciter is invited not to grasp but to sit inside. To recite the verse is, in this small way, to rest.

Section III

A duʿā from
this week

Sayyid al-Istighfār — the master of seeking forgiveness. To be said in the morning and evening.

اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ رَبِّي، لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ، خَلَقْتَنِي وَأَنَا عَبْدُكَ

Allāhumma anta rabbī, lā ilāha illā ant, khalaqtanī wa anā ʿabduk…

"O Allāh, You are my Lord — there is no god but You. You created me and I am Your servant…"

Whoever recites it with certainty in the morning and dies that day, enters Paradise; likewise for the evening.

— Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6306
